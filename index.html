About Me

I am a PhD candidate in mineral physics at the University of California, Berkeley. I received my BS in physics from California Polytechnic State University, San Luis Obispo in 2019.

My doctoral research investigates how extreme pressure states affect the mechanisms governing thermal transport in condensed matter materials. I specialize in experiments using ultrafast pump-probe spectroscopy with diamond anvil cell techniques to measure the thermal conductivity of complex natural alloys and dielectrics. I am currently contemplating when in Earth's history the inner core nucleated, how Earth’s magnetic field might have persisted for billions of years before its nucleation, and the resulting impact on global heat flow.

My undergraduate research focused on the development of a novel radio detector sensitive to air showers generated by the highest energy earth-skimming tau neutrinos under the advisement of Dr. Stephanie Wissel at Cal Poly, SLO.

Outside of academia, I enjoy playing soccer, reading lit-fic, and exploring the Bay Area's nature, food, and music scenes. I first became interested in math at age seven when I started playing poker with my grandfather, betting mostly with pennies and dimes. Growing up, I regularly watched Bill Nye the Science Guy and ate turkey and cheese sandwiches with my grandmother, nurturing a lifelong appreciation for a good sandwich and the scientific process.
